## Goal

The new world `src/middle_earth/worlds/feature_warehouse.world` (world name
`warehouse_irregular`, a 64.3 m irregular loop with 99 cone gates 2.2 m wide and no
fiducials) must support RTAB-Map **mapping with loop closures** and **localization in a
saved map**, as reliably as the existing racetrack warehouse. Today it gets **zero accepted
loop closures**. Fix the world first; change RTAB-Map parameters only if the world fix is
not enough, and never in a way that regresses the racetrack.

## Hard constraints

- **No fiducials.** No ArUco/AprilTag markers, QR-like patterns or other fiducial textures,
  anywhere: the point of this world is tag-free localization.
- **Keep the course geometry.** The centreline, the cone gates (names `cone_gate_NNN_a/b`

  re-run, diff the cone poses and the sidecar before and after; they must be identical.

  centreline, as now (the sidecar records `minimum_cluster_center_distance_m`). Decorations
  added *on* existing surfaces (textures, thin panels without collision) are fine.
- **Do not touch the car.** `src/middle_earth/models/rc_ackermann_vehicle` and
  `oak_d_lite` (physics, frames, sensors) are out of scope.
- **Deterministic and self-contained.** The world is generated by
  `src/middle_earth/scripts/build_feature_warehouse.py` (seeded RNG). Keep it that way:
  change the generator, regenerate, and commit the generator, the world, any texture files

  `full_sim_saurcon.launch.py` for the resource path). They must not be downloaded at run
  time. Using the Gazebo Fuel models the racetrack world already uses is acceptable (they
  are cached), but prefer committed assets.
- **Do not modify or discard other work in the tree.** The working tree contains

The onboard images (see `evaluation/cones_gz/fw_mapping/frames/`) show why: every
surface is a flat-shaded colour. The world has **no textures at all**: 1,055
`<material>` blocks with only `<ambient>`/`<diffuse>`, and no albedo maps. Specifically:

1. **Floor.** A single grey box (0.43 grey), plus 250 "concrete patches" that are tiny
   (0.08–0.5 m × 0.03–0.2 m), 1 mm thick, and flat grey 0.29–0.53. At the camera's grazing
   angle (OAK-D is 0.137 m above the floor) they are a few pixels tall and low contrast,
   and they give almost no trackable corners.
2. **Walls.** Four plain boxes (0.60/0.64/0.68), 2.8 m high, 14.5–17.5 m from the centre:
   uniform areas, no corners except their edges.
3. **Storage clusters.** 40 cabinets, each one of four flat colours (palette repeats
   every 4 clusters), with seven tiny grey inset panels per face, three plain cartons and two
   posts. They give some corners, but they are similar from cluster to cluster. The
   repeated palette and similar silhouettes also make look-alike places, which is the
   false-loop-closure risk chapter 4 describes.
4. **Lighting.** Ambient 0.65 and one directional light **without shadows**, so faces of
   the same colour render nearly identical and edges have little contrast.
5. **Background.** The sky/background renders flat white above the walls.

RTAB-Map's visual matching (`Vis/MinInliers` = 20 in
`src/saurcon_nav/launch/rtabmap_sim.launch.py`) cannot find 20 inliers between two views of
such images, so every loop-closure candidate is rejected and the map drifts with odometry
alone. Localization in a saved map needs the same visual matches, so it will fail for the
same reason.

## Recommended approach

### 1. Measure first (before changing anything)

Write a small, reusable probe, e.g. `scripts/sim_eval/feature_probe.py`. Given a run
directory, it reports per-frame GFTT and ORB keypoint counts on `frames/*.jpg`
(percentiles), and optionally the fraction of the image area with local contrast above a
threshold. Run it on `evaluation/cones_gz/fw_mapping` and
`evaluation/cones_gz/classical_slam_astra` (racetrack) to reproduce the table above. This
is your metric for step 2: **target a median of at least 300 GFTT corners per frame** around
the whole loop, comparable to the racetrack. Also check the worst stretch of the loop, not
only the average: loop closure needs features where the car revisits.

### 2. Fix the world (primary)

All changes go into `build_feature_warehouse.py`; then regenerate.

- **Textured floor.** Give the floor a PBR material with an albedo map: a concrete texture
  with real high-frequency detail (aggregate, stains, cracks, expansion joints every few
  metres). The texture's scale must give detail at 1–5 m from a camera 0.137 m high. Tile it
  without obvious repetition (large texture, or a few variants on floor tiles). Generate it
  procedurally and deterministically if you can (e.g. NumPy noise plus drawn cracks, saved as
  PNG by the generator), so it is reproducible without downloads. It must not contain
  anything fiducial-like (no high-contrast squares or grids that resemble tags).
- **Textured walls.** Give the walls brick, block or corrugated-panel textures with distinct
  sections (doors, vents, pipes, signs with *words*, different panel colours per wall
  segment). Make each wall and each segment of a wall look different, so the four sides
  cannot be confused.
- **Textured clusters.** Apply cardboard, wood-pallet, painted-metal and label textures to
  cartons, pallets and cabinets. Vary them per cluster, not just in a 4-colour cycle, so that
  no two clusters look alike from the track. Consider adding textured Fuel assets of the kind
  the racetrack uses (`Pallet_Rack`, `Pallet_Standard`, `55gal_Drum`, `Cable_Spool`,
  `Packing_Station`, `Forklift`, `Hand_Cart`, `Dumpster_Large`, `Ladder_01`, `Pallet_Jack`
  from `fuel.gazebosim.org/1.0/OpenRobotics/models/...`), placed at the existing cluster
  centres. They are what gives the racetrack its 300–470 corners per frame. Keep the
  2.64 m clearance.
- **Lighting.** Turn on shadows for the sun (`<cast_shadows>true`), lower the ambient
  (e.g. 0.3–0.4), and consider a couple of point or spot lights so faces of different
  orientation render with different brightness.
- **Background.** Make sure the camera never looks at flat white. Walls of sufficient height
  (or a ceiling with structure: beams, lights) fill the upper image.
- **Do not add anything inside the 2.2 m corridor** or change cone colours: the cone
  detector (`src/saurcon_rl/saurcon_rl/cones.py`) thresholds hue 15–32 with high
  saturation and value. **No new texture may contain saturated orange or yellow** in that
  band, or the detector will see phantom cones. Check the detector's false-positive count
  on new onboard frames: it must be 0 off the cones (see "Validation", step 3).

### 3. Parameters (secondary, only if the world fix is insufficient)

If the probe meets its target but closures are still rejected, examine RTAB-Map's own
feature counts (`/rtabmap/info` statistics: `Keypoint/Current_frame/words`,
`Loop/Visual_inliers`). Only then consider, in `rtabmap_sim.launch.py`:

- `Kp/MaxFeatures` and `Vis/MaxFeatures` (more features extracted);
- `Vis/FeatureType` or `Kp/DetectorStrategy` (e.g. ORB vs GFTT/BRIEF);
- `GFTT/QualityLevel`, `GFTT/MinDistance`.

Do **not** lower `Vis/MinInliers` below 20 or relax `RGBD/OptimizeMaxError` (3.0) to "get
closures". Chapter 4 documents a false closure that moved the racetrack map 4 m; the guards
are deliberate. Any parameter change must be re-validated on the racetrack (step 4 of
Validation) with no loss of accepted closures and no rise in map error.
